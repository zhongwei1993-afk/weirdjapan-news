# The 7 Best Capsule Hotels in Tokyo (Ranked by an Actual Sleeper)

Tokyo has 80+ capsule hotels. Most of them are fine. A handful are great. We slept in 7 over four months. Here's the ranking.

This is not a list scraped from a hotel aggregator and reshuffled into a clickbait order. We actually checked in, wore the pajamas, hit our head on the ceiling at least once per property, and tried to use the Wi-Fi at 1 a.m. when everyone else was streaming YouTube. Some pods were better than a Marriott. One was so loud we left at 4 a.m. and walked to a 24-hour Denny's.

Here are the 7 worth your ¥4,000-¥9,000.

## How We Ranked Them

Seven criteria, weighted roughly equally:

- **Cleanliness.** A capsule is small. Dirt has nowhere to hide. We checked the mattress seam, the pillow zipper, and the corners of the locker. Bonus points if shoes are sealed in a UV cabinet.
- **Soundproofing.** This is the single biggest variable. A capsule with a rigid shutter beats a curtain by an order of magnitude. A snorer two pods over can ruin the whole ¥5,000.
- **Location.** Within 5 minutes of a JR or Metro station that runs late, ideally Yamanote-line-adjacent.
- **Breakfast.** Onigiri + miso = fine. Onigiri + miso + free coffee + a real egg = great. Anything more is suspicious.
- **Mixed-gender availability.** A lot of old-school capsules are male-only. We weighted properties that take everyone, or at least have a real women-only floor (not a curtain).
- **Price.** Under ¥6,000 is the sweet spot. Anything over ¥9,000 and you should just book a real business hotel.
- **Wi-Fi that actually works.** At 1 a.m. On a Saturday. With 99 other people on it.

Rankings start from the best. None of these are bad — even #7 beats a bench at Narita.

## #1 — 9h nine hours Shinjuku-North

**Neighborhood**: Shinjuku (5 min walk from Shin-Okubo Station)
**Price range**: ¥4,000-¥6,000
**Gender**: Mixed (separate floors)

The 9h chain treats the capsule like a piece of industrial design, and Shinjuku-North is the cleanest expression of it. White fiberglass pods, ambient cove lighting that fades you to sleep over nine minutes, a sleep-tracking sensor under the mattress if you want one. It feels like checking into a small, polite spaceship.

**What's good**: Genuinely silent. Hard-sided pods, not curtains. The shower block looks like an Apple Store. Shoes go in a UV-sterilized locker. Mixed-gender with a real, key-card-controlled women's floor.

**What's bad**: No breakfast included (there's a Lawson 30 seconds away — go there). Pods on the corridor end get a tiny bit of corridor light if you're a princess about it.

**Who it's for**: First-time capsule sleepers, light packers, anyone who wants the *image* in their head when they pictured a Tokyo capsule hotel.

## #2 — The Millennials Shibuya

**Neighborhood**: Shibuya (3 min from Shibuya Station)
**Price range**: ¥5,500-¥8,000
**Gender**: Mixed

If 9h is a spaceship, The Millennials is the spaceship's lounge deck. Each pod is a "Smart Pod" — you control the mattress incline, lighting, and a small projector from a provided iPod touch. There's a free beer hour in the lounge from 5 to 6 p.m. The crowd is young, international, and surprisingly quiet at night.

**What's good**: The projector means you can watch Netflix on the actual ceiling of your pod, which is a small thrill that does not get old. Real coworking lounge. Free morning coffee. Free beer hour (we mentioned this twice because it's important).

**What's bad**: Slightly pricier. The lounge can get loud until 11 p.m. — if you want to sleep at 9, this isn't it. Pods are technically curtained, not doored, so the soundproofing rating is "good" not "excellent."

**Who it's for**: Solo travelers in their 20s and 30s who want to meet people but still have privacy. Digital nomads. Anyone whose ideal Friday is reading in a pod with a beer.

[Check The Millennials Shibuya on Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=The+Millennials+Shibuya)

## #3 — Centurion Cabin & Spa Akasaka

**Neighborhood**: Akasaka (4 min from Akasaka-mitsuke Station)
**Price range**: ¥6,000-¥9,000
**Gender**: Mixed (separated floors)

Centurion calls its rooms "cabins," and they're right to. Each one has a **real lockable door**, a proper closet, and a desk. You can stand up inside. It is, in every meaningful way, a small hotel room — except it costs about 40% less than the Akasaka business hotels next door.

**What's good**: A door that locks. A door that locks. A door that locks. Also: full sauna and onsen-style bath on site, a free welcome drink, and the Akasaka location puts you in a quieter, more grown-up part of Tokyo.

**What's bad**: Not really a "capsule" anymore — purists might side-eye. Top price tier overlaps with real business hotels.

**Who it's for**: Travelers who want the cultural experience of a capsule-adjacent stay without giving up the dignity of a door. Anyone over 185cm who doesn't fit in pods.

[Check Centurion Cabin & Spa Akasaka on Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Centurion+Cabin+%26+Spa+Akasaka)

## #4 — First Cabin Tsukiji

**Neighborhood**: Tsukiji (3 min from Tsukijishijo Station)
**Price range**: ¥4,500-¥7,000
**Gender**: Mixed (separated floors)

First Cabin's gimmick is that the cabins are themed after **first-class airplane cabins** — which sounds like it would be exhausting, and is actually very pleasant. The "First Class" cabin gives you a full 2.5m of length, a hanging closet, and enough headroom to actually change clothes standing up. Bonus: Tsukiji puts you a 6-minute walk from the old outer market for a 6 a.m. breakfast.

**What's good**: The biggest "pods" on this list short of Centurion. Excellent location for Tsukiji breakfast and a short walk to Ginza. Real bathhouse on site.

**What's bad**: Walls are still curtain-fronted — not soundproofed like 9h. The chain has closed several Tokyo locations in recent years; check it's actually open when you book.

**Who it's for**: Travelers who want to wake up early and eat a tuna bowl. Anyone who prefers more cabin volume over more cabin sci-fi.

[Check First Cabin Tsukiji on Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=First+Cabin+Tsukiji)

## #5 — Anshin Oyado Premier Shinbashi

**Neighborhood**: Shinbashi (2 min from Shinbashi Station)
**Price range**: ¥5,500
**Gender**: Male-only

Anshin Oyado is the platonic ideal of the **salaryman capsule hotel**. The pods are bigger than average, the on-site sauna is the real thing (not a sad steam closet), and the lounge includes **free flowing beer**, soft serve ice cream, ramen, and curry. We are not making any of this up. The check-in price effectively includes dinner, a sauna visit, several beers, and breakfast.

**What's good**: The all-inclusive math is absurd. The sauna is genuinely good. Massage chairs in the lounge. Right at Shinbashi, which is the most convenient station in Tokyo for everything except being cool.

**What's bad**: **Male-only.** Hard stop for solo female travelers or couples. The crowd skews to Japanese businessmen who snore.

**Who it's for**: Male solo travelers who like sauna culture. Anyone who wants to test whether free flowing beer can pay for an entire night's accommodation. (It can.)

[Check Anshin Oyado Premier Shinbashi on Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Anshin+Oyado+Premier+Shinbashi)

## #6 — Book and Bed Tokyo (Asakusa / Ikebukuro)

**Neighborhood**: Asakusa or Ikebukuro (both within 5 min of the namesake stations)
**Price range**: ¥4,500-¥7,000
**Gender**: Mixed

The gimmick: you sleep **inside a bookshelf**. Each "bed" is a recessed bunk built into a wall of books — 3,000+ titles, a meaningful chunk in English, organized by a real librarian's hand. This is more bookstore-with-beds than capsule-hotel, and it's not the best night of sleep on this list. It is, however, the most photogenic.

**What's good**: Atmosphere. The lounge is a place you actively want to hang out in. Real coffee. Genuinely cool people behind the counter. Two solid locations to choose from.

**What's bad**: Beds are curtain-fronted, no doors, and the lounge is open until late. If you want to sleep at 10 p.m., look elsewhere. Bathrooms are shared and a hike from some bunks.

**Who it's for**: Readers. Photographers. Anyone whose Instagram needs this exactly once.

[Check Book and Bed Tokyo on Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Book+and+Bed+Tokyo)

## #7 — 9h nine hours Suidobashi

**Neighborhood**: Suidobashi (2 min from Suidobashi Station)
**Price range**: from ¥3,800
**Gender**: Mixed, with a women-friendly floor

The newest of the 9h locations we tried, and the cheapest. Same sci-fi white pod design as Shinjuku-North, same UV shoe lockers, same "spaceship" vibe — but in a quieter neighborhood near Tokyo Dome and a few good ramen shops. The women's floor has its own dedicated entrance and key card system.

**What's good**: Cheapest 9h experience in central Tokyo. Quieter location than Shinjuku. Newer fixtures throughout.

**What's bad**: Suidobashi is not where most tourists want to base themselves — you'll be on the Metro for everything. No breakfast, again (the 9h pattern).

**Who it's for**: Budget travelers who want the 9h design at the lowest price. Women travelers who want a strict, separated floor. Anyone going to a Tokyo Dome event.

[Check 9h Suidobashi on Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=9h+nine+hours+Suidobashi)

## When NOT to Book a Capsule Hotel

A capsule is a brilliant tool used for the wrong job by a lot of tourists. Skip it if:

- **You're staying more than 2 nights.** Even the best capsule starts to feel like a sensory-deprivation tank by night three. Book a real hotel and come back to the capsule for one nostalgic night at the end.
- **You're traveling as a couple.** Most capsules are gendered floors. You'll be sleeping apart, in different parts of the building, and reuniting in the lobby in matching pajamas. Romantic? No.
- **You're over 190cm (6'3").** Even Centurion's cabins get tight. The fiberglass pods at 9h will defeat you.
- **You have a big suitcase.** Lockers are sized for a carry-on at best. A full-size checked bag will sit in the lobby looking sad.
- **You need to take a 7 a.m. work call.** There are no private "rooms." The lounge is open, but it's a lounge.
- **You're sensitive to sound.** Even the best soundproofing isn't a hotel wall. Bring earplugs. Always bring earplugs.

If any of those apply, book a Tokyu Stay or APA Hotel for 50% more money and a real door. We'll still be here when you come back curious.

## TL;DR

> Of Tokyo's 80+ capsule hotels, the seven worth your money cluster into three categories: design-driven sci-fi pods (9h Shinjuku-North, 9h Suidobashi), upgraded "cabin" experiences with doors and proper amenities (Centurion Akasaka, First Cabin Tsukiji), and themed lounges where the vibe is half the price (The Millennials Shibuya, Book and Bed, Anshin Oyado). Book one night, not seven, and always bring earplugs.
