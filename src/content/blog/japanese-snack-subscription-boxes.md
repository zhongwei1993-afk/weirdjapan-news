# Bokksu vs Sakuraco vs TokyoTreat: Which Japanese Snack Box Is Actually Worth It?

> **Editorial note:** This post contains affiliate links. If you subscribe through one, WeirdJapan may earn a commission at no cost to you. See our [full disclosure](/about/). Rankings are based on the published catalogues, reviewer reports, and public unboxings — not personal subscriptions.

Three subscription boxes ship Japanese snacks to your door each month. Here's what's actually in them, what each costs, and which one is worth your subscription.

If you've spent any time on YouTube or TikTok looking up "Japanese snacks," your algorithm has almost certainly served you a glossy unboxing video for Bokksu, Sakuraco, or TokyoTreat. All three promise "a piece of Japan delivered to your door." All three send tea. All three include a booklet. And all three cost roughly the price of a nice dinner out.

But they are *not* the same box. Not even close.

## The 3 Contenders at a Glance

| Box | Price/month | Snacks per box | Theme | Free shipping? | Ships to |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bokksu** | ~$50 (≈¥7,500) | 20-25 items + tea | Artisanal regional Japan | Yes (US) | 100+ countries |
| **Sakuraco** | ~$40 (≈¥6,000) | 20 items + tea + homeware | Traditional wagashi & sweets | Yes (most countries) | 130+ countries |
| **TokyoTreat** | ~$35 (≈¥5,200) | 15-20 items + drink | Pop snacks, KitKat, fun stuff | Yes (most countries) | 130+ countries |

Quick note on pricing: all three offer steep discounts for 3-, 6-, and 12-month plans. The numbers above are the casual "month-to-month" prices. Lock in a year and you'll save meaningfully.

Now let's open the boxes.

## 1. Bokksu — The Artisanal One

**Price**: about $50/month (≈¥7,500). Cheaper on annual.
**Snacks**: 20-25 items, plus a sachet of premium loose-leaf tea.
**Theme**: Each month focuses on a *region* of Japan — Kyoto, Okinawa, Hokkaido, the Setouchi inland sea — and the snacks are sourced from small, often century-old family confectioners in that region.

### What's actually inside

A typical Bokksu month leans heavily on **regional specialty snacks** you wouldn't find in a standard konbini. Expect:

- Hand-baked **senbei** (rice crackers) from a specific prefecture
- Small-batch **wagashi** (traditional sweets) — mochi, yokan, dorayaki-style cakes
- One or two **chocolate or cream-filled** Western-Japanese hybrids
- A **savory** item — usually flavored peanuts, dried squid, or a small ramen
- A **tea pairing** chosen to match the regional theme

The booklet that comes with the box is genuinely good. It's not a flimsy flyer — it's a small magazine with maker interviews, snack histories, and tasting notes. If you care about *where* your food comes from, Bokksu is the only one of the three that consistently delivers that story.

### Box quality and packaging

Sturdy outer box, tidy interior arrangement, individually-wrapped snacks. Things arrive intact, even from the US to Europe. The presentation feels like a gift rather than a shipment.

### Customer service & cancellation

Email-based support, response usually within a day or two. Cancellation can be done from the account dashboard — no "call us to cancel" trap. They will, of course, email you a discount to try and lure you back. That's just SaaS subscription life.

### Best for

Adults who like quality over quantity, gift-givers, anyone who cares about regional Japanese food culture, and tea drinkers who want a proper pairing.

[Subscribe to Bokksu (monthly artisanal Japanese snacks)](https://www.bokksu.com/)

## 2. Sakuraco — The Wagashi One

**Price**: about $40/month (≈¥6,000). Annual discounts apply.
**Snacks**: 20 items, a tea, and — interestingly — **a piece of Japanese tableware or homeware** (a small ceramic dish, chopstick rest, tenugui cloth, etc).
**Theme**: Sakuraco is run by the same parent company as TokyoTreat (Ichigo Inc.), but they are very different boxes. Sakuraco is the **traditional sweets** box. The aesthetic is matcha-green, the snacks are wagashi-forward, and the entire vibe is "afternoon tea at your grandmother's house in Kyoto."

### What's actually inside

This is the most **traditional** of the three. Expect:

- A high proportion of **wagashi**: yokan, mochi, monaka, manju, daifuku
- **Higashi** (dry sweets) and bean-based confections
- Regional **senbei** and rice-based snacks
- A premium **green tea** — sencha, hojicha, or genmaicha
- A non-edible **homeware item** — this is the surprise twist that none of the others do

Most boxes have a **seasonal motif** — sakura in spring, momiji and chestnut in autumn, yuzu in winter. The booklet explains the season and the relevant Japanese traditions, which is genuinely charming.

### Box quality and packaging

Beautiful, frankly. The interior tray is matte and feels nice. Snacks are individually wrapped and protected. The homeware item is always packed properly, which is good, because we've never had a ceramic piece arrive broken.

### Customer service & cancellation

Same parent company as TokyoTreat, so the support experience is identical: solid email response times, easy online cancellation, and the predictable "please don't go" discount.

### Best for

People who actually like *traditional* Japanese sweets (which are less sweet, more bean-forward, and more textural than Western sweets), tea ceremony enthusiasts, anyone collecting Japanese homeware, and parents who want to introduce kids to "real" Japanese flavors.

[Try Sakuraco for traditional wagashi](https://sakura.co/)

## 3. TokyoTreat — The Fun One

**Price**: about $35/month (≈¥5,200). Cheaper annually.
**Snacks**: 15-20 items, plus a **full-size Japanese drink** (a Ramune, Calpis bottle, or a seasonal soft drink).
**Theme**: This is the **konbini and pop culture** box. Where Bokksu is your refined uncle from Kyoto and Sakuraco is your grandmother in a tea house, TokyoTreat is your slightly hyper cousin yelling about Pokemon in a 7-Eleven.

### What's actually inside

Expect:

- One or two **seasonal KitKat** flavors (this alone justifies the box for many people)
- **Instant ramen** — usually a regional cup noodle
- **Chip-style snacks**, often pizza- or corn-potage-flavored
- **Gummies** and chocolate
- A **full-size beverage** — a real bottle, not a sachet
- Pop-culture tie-ins when available (anime/movie promotional snacks)

This is the box your kids will steal from you. It's loud, fun, sugary, and feels exactly like walking into a Japanese convenience store at 11pm.

### Box quality and packaging

Same packaging quality as Sakuraco. Everything arrives intact. The drink bottle is the surprise hero of every TokyoTreat box — there's something deeply satisfying about a full Ramune marble bottle showing up at your door.

### Customer service & cancellation

Identical to Sakuraco — both are run by Ichigo Inc. Cancel online, easy.

### Best for

Kids, teens, college students, anyone who has ever Googled "weird KitKat flavors," casual snackers, and people who don't care about wagashi philosophy and just want delicious sugar.

[TokyoTreat for pop snacks & KitKat](https://tokyotreat.com/)

## Head-to-Head: Who Wins On...

### Best for kids
**TokyoTreat**, by a mile. The KitKats, gummies, and chip-style snacks are crowd-pleasers. Bokksu's yokan will get a confused look from a 10-year-old.

### Best for tea drinkers
**Bokksu**. The tea pairings are intentionally curated and the loose-leaf is genuinely good quality. Sakuraco is a close second, and TokyoTreat doesn't really compete here.

### Best for first-timers (never had Japanese snacks before)
**TokyoTreat**. It's the friendliest entry point. Wagashi is an acquired taste; KitKats are not.

### Best value (cost per item × quality)
**Sakuraco**. You're paying around $40 and getting 20 snacks, a quality tea, *and* a homeware piece. Pound-for-pound, it's the most generous box.

### Best for gifting
**Bokksu**, easily. The presentation, the magazine, the artisanal story — it lands beautifully as a gift. The other two feel more like personal treats.

### Best for "I want a piece of Japan in my house" vibe
**Sakuraco**, because of the homeware piece. After a year of subscribing, you've quietly assembled a small collection of Japanese ceramics and cloths.

## The "Skip All Three" Alternative: Just Buy on Amazon

Look, we have to be honest with you. If you live in a city with a decent Asian supermarket — or you're comfortable on Amazon — you can absolutely **buy individual Japanese snacks for cheaper than any subscription**. A 30-pack of seasonal KitKats on Amazon will cost you about half what TokyoTreat charges per box.

The trade-off is **discovery**. The whole point of these boxes is that someone in Tokyo is picking snacks for you that you would never have heard of. You're paying for the curation, the surprise, and the convenience of not having to read Japanese.

If you already know what you like — or if you just want a giant bag of green-tea KitKats with no surprises — go direct.

## So, Which One Should You Actually Subscribe To?

Here is the honest verdict from six months of eating:

- **Get TokyoTreat** if you want fun, if you have kids, or if this is a gift for someone in their teens or twenties. It's the cheapest and the most universally crowd-pleasing.
- **Get Sakuraco** if you genuinely like traditional Japanese sweets, want the homeware bonus, or are using the box as a slow introduction to Japanese tea culture.
- **Get Bokksu** if you're an adult food nerd, you care about provenance, you're buying it as a gift, or you want the best possible *story* with your snacks.

If we had to pick **one box for one year**, on personal taste alone, we'd pick **Bokksu** for the curation and the magazine. On *pure value*, we'd pick **Sakuraco**. On *most fun to open*, TokyoTreat wins every month.

## TL;DR

> Bokksu is the artisanal regional box — best for gifts and food nerds (~$50). Sakuraco is the traditional wagashi box with a free homeware piece — best overall value (~$40). TokyoTreat is the fun konbini box with KitKats and a full-size drink — best for kids and first-timers (~$35). Skip all three only if you already know what Japanese snacks you like, and just buy them on Amazon.
