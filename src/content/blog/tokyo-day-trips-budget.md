# 12 Tokyo Day Trips You Can Book Online for Under ¥8,000

Tokyo is exhausting. The cure is leaving for a day. Here are 12 day trips out of the city you can book for less than the price of one Tokyo dinner.

We're talking real, bookable, English-friendly tours where someone else handles the trains, the timing, and the awkward bit where you stand at a rural bus stop wondering if the kanji on the signboard refers to your village or a completely different village 90 minutes away.

## How We Picked These

Four criteria, no exceptions:

- **Under ¥8,000 per adult.** Most are well under. A couple sneak right up to the line.
- **Doable in one day from Tokyo.** Out before breakfast, back before bed. No overnight stays required.
- **English support.** Either an English-speaking guide, English-language confirmation and pickup, or an itinerary so simple you don't need a guide at all.
- **No Japanese required.** You should be able to do the whole thing with a smile, a Suica card, and Google Translate as backup.

Prices reflect 2024-2025 Klook listing ranges and shift with season, demand, and the yen's mood swings. Treat them as ballpark, not gospel.

## 1. Mt. Fuji + Five Lakes Bus Day Trip — from ~¥6,000

The classic. A coach picks you up near Shinjuku before 9am, drives you out past the Tokyo suburbs and up into the Fuji Five Lakes region. You stop at the **5th Station** of Mt. Fuji itself (weather permitting—Fuji is shy), then loop down to **Lake Kawaguchi**, **Oshino Hakkai** spring village, and usually a fruit-picking orchard or shrine.

**What's included:** round-trip coach, English-speaking guide, all entrance fees.
**Why it's worth it:** the alternative is a four-leg train-and-bus pilgrimage that costs more and burns half the day on logistics.
**Best for:** first-timers, anyone with limited Japanese, photographers who want the postcard view without the planning.

## 2. Hakone Hot Springs, Owakudani Black Eggs & Lake Ashi Cruise — from ~¥7,500

Hakone is Tokyo's official spa town. This day-trip bundle drags you through every set-piece in one slightly breathless loop: the **Hakone Ropeway** over the sulfur fumes of **Owakudani**, where you eat a famous **black-shelled egg** said to add seven years to your life (it's a regular egg boiled in volcanic spring water; the shell turns black; the inside is just an egg). Then a pirate-style **boat cruise** across Lake Ashi, sometimes a stop at the open-air sculpture museum, and an onsen foot bath before the bus home.

**Price range:** ¥7,000-¥7,800 depending on whether onsen entry is included.
**Best for:** travelers who want a "Japan greatest hits" sampler day.

> [Browse Hakone day tours on Klook](https://www.klook.com/en-US/search/?query=hakone+day+tour)

## 3. Nikko World Heritage Shrines — from ~¥7,000

Two hours north of Tokyo is **Nikko**, a town built around the gold-leafed, dragon-carved **Toshogu Shrine**—the mausoleum of the shogun who unified Japan. The complex is overwhelming in the good way: cedar forests the size of small countries, lacquered gates, monkey carvings, the works. Day tours add **Kegon Falls** (97 meters, dramatic) and **Lake Chuzenji**.

**What's included:** round-trip transport, guide, shrine entrance.
**Why it's worth it:** Nikko on your own is a multi-train logistics puzzle. The bus version is a nap with a temple at the end.
**Best for:** history nerds, autumn-leaf chasers (October-November is unreal).

> [Find Nikko day trips on Klook](https://www.klook.com/en-US/search/?query=nikko+day+trip)

## 4. Kawagoe "Little Edo" Half-Day with Kimono Rental — from ~¥4,000

Thirty minutes from Ikebukuro is **Kawagoe**, a preserved Edo-period merchant town with black-walled warehouses, a candy alley, and a wooden **Bell of Time** tower that's been ringing since the 1600s. The half-day tour packages it with a **kimono rental** so you can wander the old streets dressed like a cast member.

**Price range:** ¥3,500-¥4,500 depending on kimono tier.
**Best for:** half-day travelers, photo-obsessed couples, anyone who needs a "Japan" Instagram post by Tuesday.

> [Browse Kawagoe tours on Klook](https://www.klook.com/en-US/search/?query=kawagoe)

## 5. Yokohama Chinatown + Harbor Cruise — from ~¥3,500

Twenty-five minutes south of Tokyo by train. Yokohama is a port city with the largest **Chinatown in Japan**, a Ferris wheel built into a shopping mall, the **Cup Noodle Museum** (yes, really), and a waterfront so cinematic that Japanese romance dramas use it as a default backdrop. The day-trip combo usually pairs a Chinatown food walk with a **harbor sightseeing cruise** at sunset.

**Best for:** budget travelers, families with kids, anyone who's already done Tokyo proper and wants a change of skyline.

> [Find Yokohama day tours on Klook](https://www.klook.com/en-US/search/?query=yokohama)

## 6. Snow Monkey Park (Jigokudani) Day Trip — from ~¥7,800

Yes, you can do this in a single day from Tokyo, though it's a long one. A coach leaves Shinjuku before dawn, drives through the Nagano Alps, and deposits you at **Jigokudani Monkey Park**, where wild **Japanese macaques bathe in natural hot springs** while completely ignoring you. Most tours add a sake brewery or a quick stop at **Zenkoji Temple** on the way back.

**Price range:** ¥7,500-¥7,800. Right at the cap, but unbeatable for the iconic shot.
**Best for:** wildlife photographers, winter visitors (snow + steam + monkey = peak Japan).

> [Book a snow monkey day tour on Klook](https://www.klook.com/en-US/search/?query=snow+monkey+jigokudani)

## 7. Kamakura Big Buddha + Enoshima Beach — from ~¥5,500

An hour south of Tokyo is **Kamakura**, a temple-stuffed seaside town that used to be the capital of Japan. The headline act is the **Great Buddha of Kotoku-in**, an 11-meter bronze figure sitting outdoors since 1252. Tours pair it with the floating-shrine island of **Enoshima**, the bamboo forest of **Hokokuji**, and a stretch of Pacific coastline that surfers love and most tourists don't know exists.

**Best for:** travelers who want a temple-and-beach combo, day trippers in summer.

> [Find Kamakura day tours on Klook](https://www.klook.com/en-US/search/?query=kamakura)

## 8. Mt. Takao Easy Hike + Cable Car — from ~¥3,000

The cheapest escape on this list. **Mt. Takao** is technically inside Tokyo (it's the western edge), reachable in under an hour by Keio Line. The mountain is 599 meters of well-paved trail, a cable car for the lazy half, a Buddhist temple at the top, and—if the air's clear—a view of Mt. Fuji that doesn't require a six-hour bus ride.

**Price range:** ¥2,500-¥3,500 for guided versions; self-guided costs under ¥1,500 in train fare.
**Best for:** solo travelers, anyone needing a single morning of trees.

> [Browse Mt. Takao experiences on Klook](https://www.klook.com/en-US/search/?query=mt+takao)

## 9. Tokyo Disneyland or DisneySea 1-Day Pass — ¥8,000 cap

Disney sneaks under the limit, barely. A standard 1-day passport runs **¥7,900-¥8,400** depending on the season and which gate you pick, so book early on a weekday for the lower tier. **DisneySea** is the one that exists only in Japan; even Disney purists who've done every other park rate it as the best on Earth.

**Best for:** travelers with kids, theme-park completionists, anyone who unironically loves Duffy the Bear.

## 10. Tokyo Bay Sunset Cruise + Dinner — from ~¥6,500

This one doesn't technically leave Tokyo, but it leaves Tokyo *land*, which counts. A two-hour cruise around **Tokyo Bay** with a Japanese-style dinner (often *yakatabune* houseboat-style), passing **Rainbow Bridge**, the **Skytree**, **Odaiba**, and the **Tokyo Tower** lit up against the dusk skyline. Cheesy. Romantic. Photographs like a movie.

**Best for:** couples, anniversaries, anyone done with walking 25,000 steps a day.

> [Browse Tokyo Bay cruises on Klook](https://www.klook.com/en-US/search/?query=tokyo+bay+cruise)

## 11. Sumo Morning Practice Tour — from ~¥4,500

Sumo tournaments only happen six times a year in Japan, and only three of those are in Tokyo. But you can watch **morning practice (*asageiko*)** at a working sumo stable almost any day, with a guide who whispers the rules and the etiquette while 150-kilo athletes throw each other into a clay ring three meters away.

**Price range:** ¥4,000-¥5,500.
**Best for:** sports fans, anyone who's read a sumo Wikipedia rabbit hole.

> [Find a sumo morning practice tour on Klook](https://www.klook.com/en-US/search/?query=sumo+morning+practice)

## 12. Tsukiji Outer Market Food Tour — from ~¥7,500

The inner wholesale fish market moved to Toyosu in 2018, but the **Tsukiji outer market**—the stalls, the knife shops, the tamagoyaki men, the sea-urchin counters—is still there, still loud, and still selling the best breakfast in Tokyo. A three-hour guided food tour stops at 6-8 vendors, explains what you're eating, and means you don't have to mime "is this raw" at anyone.

**Best for:** foodies, first-day jet-lagged travelers (it starts early), anyone who wants to learn what *uni* tastes like before committing to a ¥6,000 dinner version.

## Hidden Gotchas (Read Before You Book)

A few unglamorous truths the booking pages won't shout about:

- **Lead time.** Popular tours (Mt. Fuji, snow monkeys, Disney) sell out 3-7 days ahead in spring, autumn, and any week containing a Japanese public holiday. Book early.
- **Weather and Mt. Fuji.** Fuji is visible roughly 40% of days. Most tours run rain or shine and do not refund for "Fuji didn't show up." Check the forecast 24 hours out; if the mountain is hiding, swap to Hakone.
- **Pickup points.** Bus tours often depart from a specific Shinjuku or Tokyo Station meeting point that is *not* the main JR exit. Read the confirmation email twice. Arrive 15 minutes early.
- **Cash buffer.** Even on prepaid tours, lunch stops, omiyage shops, and onsen lockers may require ¥1,000-¥3,000 in cash.
- **Shoes.** Anything involving a temple or onsen means shoes off, repeatedly. Wear slip-ons. Your feet will thank you; the monk behind you will also thank you.
- **What to bring:** passport (some tours check), water bottle, a hand towel (Japan loves hand towels), and a portable charger.
